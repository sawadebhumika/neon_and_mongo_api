The lecture highlighted that AI is not new and has been used for prediction and recommendation systems for a long time. Examples included fare prediction in Ola/Uber, house price prediction, and recommendation systems in Netflix and Amazon.
The release of the ChatGPT model by OpenAI in 2022 was a turning point, but the actual story began in 2017 with Google's research paper 'Attention is All You Need.' This paper introduced a new architecture that improved the understanding of long contexts and relationships between words, leading to better content generation.
The lecture emphasized that generative AI is not just about generating text or chatting with AI interfaces but also about controlling machines in natural language without coding knowledge, enabling automation of day-to-day tasks.

The lecture then transitioned to discussing the ideal roadmap for learning generative AI, including:
Proficiency in Python or another programming language
Understanding APIs
Working with different datasets and embeddings
Understanding vector databases
Learning RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation) systems
Exploring different frameworks for building agents (Langchain, Langraph, Lama indexes, Autogen, N8N)
Understanding MCPs (Model Context Protocols) and A2A (Agent to Agent) communication
Model fine-tuning and inferencing
Exploring generative AI in the cloud (AWS, Azure, GCP)
Understanding ML Ops or LM Ops
Building projects for resumes
